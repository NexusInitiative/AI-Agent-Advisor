# Source Map

Documentation pages were re-read on October 13, 2026; papers were checked against their abstracts and, where a figure is quoted, their full text. Foundational papers retain their original publication dates; they are not all new 2026 production findings. A row tagged *(derived)* is a mathematical or logical consequence of a cited definition, not an empirical finding. A row tagged *(procedure)* is a workflow recommendation grounded in the cited findings. Venues are named only where the paper itself states one; otherwise the entry is an arXiv preprint.

| Claim | Primary sources | Confidence | Scope and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specify task, evidence, output, and failure behavior; adapt steering to the deployed provider/model. | [OpenAI reasoning best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices), [Claude prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) | High | Provider documentation; syntax and model behavior are version-sensitive. |
| For reasoning models, keep prompts simple, avoid chain-of-thought prompting, and try zero-shot before few-shot. | [OpenAI reasoning best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices) | High | Documented for OpenAI reasoning models; not a universal result for all models. |
| Claude documentation recommends long data before the query (reporting up to 30 percent better quality in its tests) and 3–5 examples; providers' example advice differs. | [Claude prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) | Medium | Vendor-reported gains on unspecified tests; validate on your workload. |
| Models use long contexts non-uniformly; accuracy is highest for information at the beginning or end and drops for the middle. Near-perfect needle-in-a-haystack scores can coexist with large drops as length grows. | [Lost in the Middle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172) (TACL), [RULER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654) (COLM 2024) | High | Experiments on earlier models; they motivate testing placement, not a fixed rule. |
| Few-shot accuracy varies with example choice, order, and prompt format; formatting sensitivity persists with larger models and instruction tuning. | [Calibrate Before Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690) (ICML 2021), [Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12837) (EMNLP 2022), [Sclar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324) (ICLR 2024) | High | Sclar reports spreads of up to 76 accuracy points on LLaMA-2-13B; other models differ. Supports "no universal example count". |
| Instructions are easiest to test when compliance is verifiable by code. | [IFEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911) | Medium | Benchmark of 25 verifiable instruction types; supports writing checkable constraints, not a claim about any one application. |
| Structured generation constrains output shape; refusals and incomplete outputs are separate states; supported schema subsets differ by provider. | [OpenAI structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs), [Claude structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs), [Grammar-Constrained Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13971) (EMNLP 2023), [XGrammar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15100) (MLSys 2025) | High | Documentation is version-sensitive. OpenAI requires all fields to be required and emulates optional values with a null union; Anthropic reports `stop_reason: "refusal"`. Shape does not establish that values are true. |
| Format restrictions can degrade reasoning performance, more so as constraints tighten. | [Let Me Speak Freely?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442) | Medium | A single preprint; results depend on task and format. Motivates comparing schema-with-reasoning-field against free-form-then-extract (procedure). |
| Outcome and trace checks, reading transcripts, and treating a 0% pass rate on frontier models as a likely broken task. | [Anthropic: Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents) (January 9, 2026) | Medium | First-party engineering guidance with worked examples; not a controlled study. |
| Infrastructure configuration alone shifted agentic coding scores (6 percentage points between most- and least-resourced setups on Terminal-Bench 2.0, p < 0.01). | [Anthropic: Quantifying infrastructure noise](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise) (February 5, 2026) | High | One benchmark family; effect sizes do not transfer unchanged to other applications. |
| Exact binomial confidence intervals; zero-failure upper bound *(derived)*: with zero failures the one-sided exact bound solves (1 − p)^n = α, giving 5.8% at n = 50 and 0.997% at n = 299. | [NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, confidence intervals for proportions](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) | High for the method; derived for the zero-failure form | NIST documents the two-sided exact interval and does not discuss the zero-failure case; the specialization and the two figures were computed and checked numerically. Requires independent, representative sampling; not a launch criterion. |
| Evaluations are experiments: use standard errors, paired differences, and planned sample sizes rather than fixed counts. | [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) | High | Method paper; assumes questions are draws from a super-population. |
| Standard statistical inference assumes hypotheses are fixed before the data are seen; adaptively reusing the same data breaks that assumption. | [Preserving Statistical Validity in Adaptive Data Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2664) | High | Theoretical treatment of adaptive reuse; supports renewing protected evaluation sets after repeated selection (procedure). |
| LLM judges show position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases and limited reasoning ability; position bias varies by judge and by the quality gap between answers. | [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks), [Judging the Judges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791) (AACL-IJCNLP 2025) | High | Original experiments use earlier models; no universal judge family, accuracy, or recalibration interval guarantees fitness. |
| Strong judges can be near chance on hard response pairs. | [JudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784) (ICLR 2025) | High | Reported for GPT-4o-class judges on the benchmark's pairs. |
| Evaluation criteria drift as people see outputs. | [Who Validates the Validators?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272) | Medium | Qualitative user study of an evaluation-assistant interface. |
| `pass^k` is the chance all k trials of a task succeed, averaged across tasks; `pass@k` is the chance at least one succeeds. τ-bench agents succeeded on under 50% of tasks with `pass^8` under 25% in retail. The 70%/30% and 0.95^10 examples are *(derived)* under stated independence. | [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045), [Codex paper (pass@k, repeated sampling)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374) | High | Definitions and benchmark findings for the models tested; the arithmetic is not an empirical claim about any agent. |
| Accuracy varies across repeated runs even under settings expected to be deterministic (up to 15% across runs). | [Non-Determinism of "Deterministic" LLM Settings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667) | Medium | Preprint; five LLMs and eight tasks. |
| Hosted-model behavior can change between versions. | [How is ChatGPT's behavior changing over time?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009) | Medium | Preprint on March versus June 2023 releases (e.g., prime identification 84% to 51%). |
| Long context versus retrieval is a measured quality/cost choice: long context outperformed RAG on average when resourced (by 3.6% to 13.1% across three models, as reported) while RAG cost less; their routing method cut cost by 39% to 65%. | [Li et al., RAG or Long-Context LLMs?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833) (EMNLP 2024 industry track) | High | Specific 2024 models and datasets; no universal token threshold. |
| Quality can first rise and then fall as more passages are retrieved. | [Long-Context LLMs Meet RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05983) | Medium | Preprint; attributes the decline to hard negatives. |
| Whether retrieved context is sufficient is a distinct property; strong models often answer rather than abstain when it is insufficient. | [Sufficient Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037) (ICLR 2025) | High | Evaluated on specific QA datasets and models. |
| Rank fusion: Elastic documents a `rank_constant` default of 60 and states RRF requires no tuning; Bruch et al. found RRF sensitive to its parameters and a convex combination outperforming it in and out of domain. | [Elastic RRF documentation](https://www.elastic.co/docs/reference/elasticsearch/rest-apis/reciprocal-rank-fusion), [An Analysis of Fusion Functions for Hybrid Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934) | Medium | The sources disagree about tuning; treat fusion method and constants as hyperparameters to validate (procedure). |
| BM25 is a robust zero-shot baseline; re-ranking and late-interaction models were best on average at high computational cost; dense and sparse retrievers often underperformed out of domain. | [BEIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663) (NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks) | High | 2021 models; does not evaluate every modern retriever. |
| Citations are often incomplete or inaccurate: 51.5% of sentences fully supported and 74.5% of citations supporting their sentence in an audit of four generative search engines; even the best models lacked complete citation support about 50% of the time on ELI5. | [Evaluating Verifiability in Generative Search Engines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09848) (Findings of EMNLP 2023), [ALCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627) (EMNLP 2023) | High | 2023 systems; supports checking citation membership and support. |
| Tool descriptions, meaningful results, and agent-computer interface design affect agent performance. | [Anthropic: Writing tools for agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents) (September 11, 2025), [SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793) | Medium | First-party guidance plus one benchmark study; small versus compound tools must be evaluated on the task. |
| Untrusted input in a developer message gives attackers the highest control; instructions injected into retrieved data can hijack applications; design-level separation of control and data helps. | [OpenAI: Safety in building agents](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/agent-builder-safety), [Indirect prompt injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12173), [CaMeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813) | High | CaMeL solved 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security versus 84% undefended, under its stated threat model. Markup alone is not a boundary. |
| Visible chain of thought often omits the influences on an answer; it cannot replace observable audit evidence. | [Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410), [Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388) (NeurIPS 2023) | High | Chen et al. report reveal rates often below 20% for the hints tested; Turpin et al. report accuracy drops of up to 36% from biasing features. |
| Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback did not reliably improve reasoning. | [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) (ICLR 2024) | High | Reasoning tasks and models tested; external verification is the alternative (procedure). |
| Self-consistency (sampling several reasoning paths and taking the most consistent answer) raised accuracy on arithmetic and commonsense benchmarks; majority selection cannot correct an error shared by all samples *(derived)*. | [Self-Consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171) (ICLR 2023) | High | Benchmark results for models of that period; no universal temperature, sample count, or agreement threshold. |
| GEPA is a prompt-optimization candidate that outperformed GRPO by 6% on average (up to 20%) with up to 35 times fewer rollouts, and a leading prompt optimizer by over 10%. | [GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457) (ICLR 2026), [GEPA documentation](https://gepa-ai.github.io/gepa/) | High | Six tasks; no universal superiority or production gain. Protect selection and release cases (procedure). |
| Thinking, effort, and request-compatibility controls vary by model (for example, prefilled assistant turns are rejected on newer Claude models). | [Claude: steering thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking-steering-and-cost), [Claude API errors](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/errors), [Claude prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices) | High | Version-sensitive; check the deployed configuration. |
| Prompt caching reuses a shared prefix and requires the entire rendered prefix to match; maintaining a session does not guarantee a hit. | [OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching) | High | Eligibility and invalidation are provider-specific; measure actual cache use and total cost. |
| Retries need bounded backoff and jitter, and operations with side effects are unsafe to retry unless idempotent; the Claude SDK retries transient failures with exponential backoff, and a spend-cap 429 keeps failing. | [Amazon Builders' Library: Timeouts, retries, and backoff with jitter](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter), [Claude API errors](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/errors) | High | Distributed-systems practice; the recovery table applies these principles to LLM failure classes (procedure). |
| Training can improve task behavior and reasoning, not only style; for knowledge injection the cited comparisons favored retrieval over unsupervised fine-tuning, especially for less popular facts. | [OpenAI reinforcement fine-tuning](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reinforcement-fine-tuning), [Ovadia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934), [Soudani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432) | Medium | The documented OpenAI platform is winding down and closed to new users; method scope only. Use `advise-fine-tune` for implementation. |
| Agentic designs trade latency and cost for task performance; agents used about 4 times the tokens of a chat and multi-agent systems about 15 times in one vendor's data. Cost per successful task, coverage, and failure severity should be compared together *(derived accounting)*. | [Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents), [Anthropic: Multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/built-multi-agent-research-system) | Medium | Vendor-reported figures for one workload; the cost identity is accounting, not a threshold. |
