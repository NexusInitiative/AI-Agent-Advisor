# Source Map

Papers were checked against their abstracts and, wherever a figure or definition is quoted, against the full text; documentation and engineering posts were re-read on October 13, 2026. Venues are named only where the paper itself states one; otherwise the entry is an arXiv preprint. Rows tagged *(procedure)* are workflow steps grounded in the cited findings, *(derived)* marks a logical or mathematical consequence of a cited definition, and vendor-reported figures are marked as such. The debugging method itself (pin, reproduce, substitute one component, fix one thing, verify on the same cases) is the controlled-experiment procedure the evaluation literature below recommends; no source claims it is the only method.

## Method and measurement

| Claim | Sources | Confidence | Scope and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failure concentrates in identifiable stages: seven RAG failure points; 14 multi-agent failure modes in three categories from 1,600+ traces (kappa 0.88); failure is not merely a function of the underlying model. | [Seven Failure Points](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05856) (CAIN 2024), [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) | High | Case studies and trace analysis; MAST percentages are as reported in the paper. |
| Read transcripts; a 0% pass rate with frontier models is most often a broken task; CORE-Bench score rose from 42% to 95% after grading, specification and reproducibility fixes; an unspecified filepath made valid solutions fail. | [Anthropic: Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents) (January 9, 2026) | Medium | First-party engineering guidance with examples. |
| Container resource limits shifted an agentic benchmark by 6 percentage points (p < 0.01); treat resources as a controlled variable; be skeptical of gaps under 3 points until configurations match. | [Anthropic: Quantifying infrastructure noise](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/infrastructure-noise) (February 5, 2026) | High | One benchmark family. |
| Accuracy varied up to 15% across runs of settings expected to be deterministic; best-to-worst gap up to 70%; no tested model was fully repeatable; TARr@N and TARa@N agreement metrics. | [Non-Determinism of "Deterministic" LLM Settings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667) | Medium | Preprint; five LLMs, eight tasks. |
| Hosted-model behavior changed between releases (prime identification 84% to 51%; more code formatting mistakes; decreased instruction following). | [How is ChatGPT's behavior changing over time?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009) | Medium | Preprint; March versus June 2023. |
| `pass^k` is the chance all k trials succeed, averaged across tasks; agents succeeded on under 50% of tasks with `pass^8` under 25% in retail. | [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) | High | Benchmark findings for models tested. |
| Evaluations are experiments: standard errors, paired differences, planned sample sizes. | [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) | High | Method paper. |
| Zero-failure upper bound solves (1 − p)^n = 0.05: 5.8% at n = 50, 0.997% at n = 299 *(derived)*. Ten steps at 95% conditional success gives 59.9% *(derived)*. | [NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm) | High for the method; derived for the specialization | NIST documents the exact binomial interval, not the zero-failure case; figures checked numerically; requires independent, representative sampling. |

| Leakage can dramatically inflate evaluation results; a matched fresh set exposed accuracy drops up to 8% (Spearman r² = 0.36 with generation likelihood; many frontier models showed minimal overfitting); contamination is a broad reliability problem. | [Don't Make Your LLM an Evaluation Benchmark Cheater](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01964), [GSM1k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332) (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks), [Benchmark Data Contamination survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04244) | High | Math and general benchmarks; effect sizes vary. |
| Strong judges scored only slightly above chance on hard pairs; judges show position, verbosity, self-enhancement bias; position bias varies by judge and quality gap; LLM-judged summarization was highly order-sensitive. | [JudgeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784) (ICLR 2025), [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks), [Judging the Judges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791) (AACL-IJCNLP 2025), [RAG vs. GraphRAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11371) | High | Earlier models; benchmark-specific. |
| Criteria drift: grading outputs changes people's criteria. | [Who Validates the Validators?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272) | Medium | Qualitative user study. |
| Query-document relevance labels correlated weakly with downstream RAG performance. | [Evaluating Retrieval Quality in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13781) | Medium | Preprint. |
| Intrinsic self-correction without external feedback struggled and sometimes degraded reasoning; sampling-consistency checks and verification questions are published hallucination-reduction options. | [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) (ICLR 2024), [SelfCheckGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08896) (EMNLP 2023), [Chain-of-Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495) | High / Medium | CoVe is a preprint; neither is a guarantee. |
| Chain-of-thought often omits the influences on an answer (reveal rate often under 20%; accuracy drops up to 36% from biasing features). | [Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410), [Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388) (NeurIPS 2023) | High | Hint-based experiments. |

## Retrieval and grounding (R1–R8)

| Claim | Sources | Confidence | Scope and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|

## Training and adaptation (T1–T6)

| Claim | Sources | Confidence | Scope and caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| New-fact examples are learned slowly, emerge as late-stage overfitting, and raise hallucination roughly linearly; early stopping and filtering unknown examples reduce risk; highly-known-only data was not best. | [Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904) (EMNLP 2024) | High | Closed-book QA setup; verified in the paper text. |
| Retrieval outperformed unsupervised fine-tuning for knowledge, by a large margin for least popular facts. | [Ovadia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934), [Soudani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432) | Medium | Preprints. |
| Deduplication cut memorized emission ten-fold; train-test overlap exceeded 4% of validation sets; memorization rises with capacity, duplication and context; memorization varies by fine-tuning task. | [Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06499) (ACL 2022), [Carlini et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07646), [Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06714) | High / Medium | Mostly pretraining-scale results; fine-tuning study is a preprint. |
| Fine-tuning can distort pretrained features and do worse out of distribution than linear probing. | [Kumar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054) (ICLR 2022) | Medium | Vision experiments. |
| Validation loss rises after a minimum while training loss keeps falling (classical overfitting signal). | [Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville, *Deep Learning*, section 7.8](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/contents/regularization.html) | High | Textbook; general neural-network setting. |
| SFT models overfit validation loss after one epoch yet more epochs improved human preference; perplexity did not correlate with generation quality and checkpoints were chosen on a dev set. | [InstructGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155), [LIMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206) | High | Specific models and data; supports using a task metric beside loss. |
| Catastrophic forgetting in 1B–7B models, severity increasing with scale, alleviated by general instruction tuning; sequential skill learning risks forgetting; mixed strategies help. | [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08747), [Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05492) (ACL 2024) | Medium / High | Model range tested. |
| LoRA forgets with a power law in parameters and steps, not avoidable by early stopping; LoRA learns less and forgets less than full fine-tuning and underperforms on code and math in standard settings. | [Kalajdzievski](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05605), [LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673) (TMLR 2024) | High / Medium | Specific models and tasks. |
| Benign fine-tuning degrades safety to a lesser extent; ten examples under $0.20 jailbroke GPT-3.5 Turbo; mitigations discussed: data moderation, safety examples, red-team auditing, with backdoor caveat. | [Fine-tuning Aligned Language Models Compromises Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03693) | High | Preprint; verified in the paper text. |
| Narrow insecure-code fine-tuning produced broad misalignment, strongest in GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct; educational framing prevented it; behavior was inconsistent. | [Emergent Misalignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424) (earlier revision accepted at ICML 2025) | High | Specific models and data. |
| RLHF generalized better but reduced output diversity versus SFT; sycophancy is general in preference-trained assistants; over-optimizing a proxy reward hurts gold performance. | [Kirk et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06452), [Sharma et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), [Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) | High | Models and setups studied. |
| Wrong control tokens drastically worsen performance; extra special tokens hurt; apply the same chat template in training and inference. | [Hugging Face: Chat templates](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/chat_templating) | High | Documentation; version-sensitive. |
